## Key finding about post-operation WETH sponsorship

Public original [Pimlico SingletonPaymaster source](https://github.com/pimlicolabs/singleton-paymaster) states ERC20 mode **does not take an ERC20 token prefund during paymaster validation**, enabling potential post-operation collection after a liquidation. But the **deployed v0.7 contract is permissioned**: it requires a quote/signature from a recognized authorized external signer. As documented in the source, malicious nonpayment may cause fallback to the user's Pimlico balance, so nonrecourse third-party liability is **not** established by mere ERC20 mode. An apparent WETH allowance or positive paymaster deposit never grants external native ETH financing rights to NQC.

Current [Pimlico documentation](https://docs.pimlico.io/guides/getting-started) separates verifying-paymaster prepaid Pimlico balance from ERC20 token fee mode and does not guarantee this exact real mainnet deployment/policy offers this liquidator WETH credit without upfront token requirements, service deposits or collection liability. Current [Pimlico commercial site](https://www.pimlico.io/) says users need a card for pay-as-you-go; an API/signup/billing obligation paid by operator violates NQC's zero-own-capital condition unless a truly independent sponsor accepts full liability.

[Circle Paymaster](https://www.circle.com/paymaster) is USDC-only at publication and [its own integration article](https://www.circle.com/blog/how-to-integrate-circle-paymaster-to-enable-users-to-pay-gas-fees-with-their-usdc-balance) explicitly checks the *user's USDC balance before execution*. A WETH/WETH liquidation beginning with zero inventory therefore cannot be treated as zero-capital-sponsored by Circle simply by swapping WETH to USDC later in the same operation. [Alchemy Gas Manager](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/wallets/low-level-infra/gas-manager/gas-sponsorship/using-sdk/pay-gas-with-any-erc20-token) may front native ETH but still invoices its policy owner for gas and administrative fees, including cases where collection fails; no NQC policy owner liability is authorized.

## Why this is not provider admission

The exact source RMC011 execution-authorized external provider registry remains `provider_count=0` and `providers=[]`. Existence of a deployed paymaster with ETH deposited does not establish any of the following: actual operator legal identity and authorized signers; live usable WETH fee quote; collateral/credit limit allocated to NQC; whether signer/API agrees to front ETH with **no operator deposit, credit card, invoice or recourse**; who pays gas on postOp/reverted or invalidated operations; real wallet compatibility and signed UserOperation; EntryPoint/bundler inclusion; ex-ante ability to capture opportunities; Nexus realized P&L; RMC011 terminal authority; RMC017 Census closure.

A true positive requires independent provider-originated, transaction-specific signed offer with Ethereum mainnet chain and EntryPoint binding, executable operation bytes, approved WETH fee token and collection policy, proof of available **exclusive unencumbered native deposit/quota**, expiry and caps, signature validation, successful and reverted simulated production-account UserOperations, safe postOp and operator-sweep logic, **nonrecourse liability for all failed gas**, at least two independent chain observations, and a separate capital-source authority audit. This test intentionally performs **NONE** of those off-chain commercial actions.

The prior real EntryPoint v0.7 NQC fork [PR #641](https://github.com/josuechavando350-png/nexus-engine/pull/641) used entirely **Foundry test-minted ETH** for the test paymaster and bundler. This new PR only inspects an actually deployed, externally operated-design contract, without transferring from or pretending to own its funds. No main branch changes, no production transaction, user money, wallet/key creation, subscription, provider account or paid infrastructure.
