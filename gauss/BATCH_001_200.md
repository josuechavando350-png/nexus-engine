## The additional 84 operators

- 12 exact bounded graph-combinatorics algorithms: connected components, bipartite coloring, spanning-tree count, topological-order count, maximum clique, minimum dominating set, proper-coloring count, Hamiltonian path, simple path count, fixed-length walk count, maximum general matching, and girth.
- 12 discrete signal-processing algorithms: complex direct/inverse DFT, linear/circular convolution, cross-correlation, sample autocovariance, FIR and stable first-order IIR filtering, Hann-windowed STFT, power periodogram, Goertzel bin, and Haar transform.
- 12 descriptive statistical algorithms: weighted central moments, covariance, Pearson/Spearman/Kendall correlation, median absolute deviation, weighted quantile, empirical CDF, two-sample KS, Mann–Whitney U, Gini coefficient, and contingency chi-square statistic.
- 12 polynomial algorithms: Horner evaluation, analytic derivative/integral, multiplication/division, barycentric and Newton interpolation, quadratic real roots, composition, translation, Bernstein conversion, and finite-difference table.
- 12 bounded integer arithmetic algorithms: Möbius function, divisor sum/count, multiplicative order, prime-field discrete log and square roots, Jacobi symbol, linear congruence, Diophantine witness, continued fractions, Farey sequence, and primitive Pythagorean triples.
- 12 bounded structural graph algorithms: transitive closure/reduction, weighted DAG longest path, directed simple cycles and feedback vertex set, global edge connectivity, eccentricities, graph center, independence polynomial, triangle count, exact rooted arborescence count, and k-core decomposition.
- 12 finite-alphabet information algorithms: KL/JS divergence, total variation, Hellinger, Bhattacharyya, Chernoff, discrete Wasserstein-1, Bayes posterior, verified stationary Markov entropy rate, conditional mutual information, channel capacity, and conditional entropy.

Every algorithm has its own exported implementation, declared domain, unique ID, input and result contract, bounded supported domain, and explicit rejection of invalid inputs. Exponential procedures reject oversized problems; numerical procedures reject nonfinite values. Some outputs are numerical approximations or supplied-model quantities, not universal mathematical or real-world guarantees. Operators that share mathematical identities remain separate only where their contracts, algorithmic computations, and outputs are materially distinct; a preset or ID alias alone never counts.

## Evidence and verification

`gauss/core/registry.mjs` imports the original 116 from `registry-prebatch.mjs` without changing their algorithm implementations, appends the 84 from `batch-200-additions.mjs`, and rejects duplicate IDs, duplicate executable functions, missing domains, or an incorrect 200 count. The fixture `gauss/fixtures/selftest-problem.json` contains one task for each registry ID. `gauss/tests/batch-200-evidence.test.mjs` proves complete 200/200 execution and independently replays each result through WALLE; each of the 84 newly added outputs is separately forged with recalculated task and report SHA-256 hashes and must be rejected. `batch-200-new-operators.test.mjs`, `batch-200-randomized-oracles.test.mjs`, and `batch-200-input-contracts.test.mjs` exercise independent analytical/exhaustive oracles, seeded crosschecks, malformed inputs, and finite JSON outputs. All prior tests remain intact.

NEXUS orchestrates the fixture into GAUSS; the reference Ising task invokes the **classical** Nexus Quantum statevector simulator, whose problem-bound receipt must agree with the GAUSS Ising solution. WALLE independently replays the full fixture. No physical QPU, quantum advantage, scientific universality, commercial outcome, or deployment to a customer is implied.
